## The key idea

1. **Find the key.** If it is in an **internal node**, replace it with its **predecessor** (the largest key in its left subtree), then delete the predecessor from its leaf.
2. **Remove it from the leaf.** If the leaf still has at least ⌈m/2⌉ − 1 keys, you are done.
3. **Underflow?** Look at the **left sibling first, then the right**:
   - **Borrow** if a sibling has more than the minimum: the parent's separating key moves **down**, and the sibling's nearest key moves **up**.
   - **Merge** if neither can spare one: the node, the separating key and a sibling join into one node. The parent loses a key, so check it too.

## Worked example

Order 3 (at least 1 key, except the root).

**Borrow.** Root `[20]` with leaves `[8]` and `[28 | 30]`. **Delete 8.**

1. The left leaf is now empty: **underflow**. It has no left sibling.
2. The right sibling `[28 | 30]` can spare a key. **20 moves down**, **28 moves up**.
3. Result: root `[28]` with leaves `[20]` and `[30]`.

**Merge.** Root `[20]` with leaves `[8]` and `[30]`. **Delete 8.**

1. The left leaf is empty and `[30]` has nothing to spare.
2. Merge the empty leaf, **20** and `[30]` into `[20 | 30]`.
3. The root is now empty, so `[20 | 30]` becomes the **root**. The tree is one level shorter.

## Watch out

- **Borrow through the parent.** Moving 28 straight into the empty leaf would put it left of 20, which breaks the order.
- **Check the left sibling first.** Only use the right one if the left can't spare a key or doesn't exist.

## Check yourself

Order 3. Root `[20 | 40]` with leaves `[5]`, `[25]` and `[50 | 60]`. **Delete 25.** What does the tree look like?

## Answer

The middle leaf underflows. Its left sibling `[5]` has only the minimum, so check the right: `[50 | 60]` can spare one. **40 moves down** and **50 moves up**: root `[20 | 50]` with leaves `[5]`, `[40]` and `[60]`.
