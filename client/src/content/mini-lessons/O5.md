## The key idea

When a key lands in a **full leaf**, the leaf **overflows** (it holds m keys, one too many). Split it:

1. Find the **middle key**. If there are two middle keys (m is even), take the **left** one.
2. Keys **left of the middle** stay in one node, keys **right of it** go to a new node.
3. The **middle key moves up** into the parent, between the two new nodes.

## Worked example

Order 3 (at most 2 keys). Root `[20]` with leaves `[5 | 12]` and `[25 | 30]`. **Insert 8.**

1. 8 belongs in `[5 | 12]`, which becomes `[5 | 8 | 12]`: **3 keys, overflow**.
2. The middle key is **8**.
3. Split into `[5]` and `[12]`, and move **8 up** into the root.

Result: root `[8 | 20]` with leaves `[5]`, `[12]` and `[25 | 30]`. The root has 2 keys and 3 children, so the tree is valid.

**Even order:** with order 4, `[10 | 20 | 30 | 40]` splits into `[10]` and `[30 | 40]`, and **20** moves up.

## Watch out

- **Promote the middle key, not the key you inserted.** They are only the same by coincidence, as with 8 above.
- **The middle key moves, it isn't copied.** It leaves the leaf completely.

## Check yourself

Order 3. Root `[20]` with leaves `[5 | 12]` and `[25 | 30]`. **Insert 35.** What does the tree look like?

## Answer

`[25 | 30 | 35]` overflows and **30** moves up. Root `[20 | 30]` with leaves `[5 | 12]`, `[25]` and `[35]`.
