## The key idea

A new key **always goes into a leaf**. First search for it, as if it were already in the tree. The leaf where the search ends is where it goes, in sorted order.

If that leaf still has room (fewer than m − 1 keys), you are **done**. Nothing else in the tree changes.

## Worked example

Order 3 (at most 2 keys). Root `[20 | 40]` with leaves `[5 | 12]`, `[25]` and `[50 | 60]`. **Insert 27.**

1. Root: 27 is between 20 and 40, so go to the **middle leaf**.
2. `[25]` has 1 key and room for 2. Put 27 in sorted order: `[25 | 27]`.
3. Done. The root and the other leaves are unchanged.

## Watch out

- **Don't insert into the root** just because the key "fits between" its keys. Keep going down until you reach a leaf.
- **Keep the leaf sorted.** Inserting 22 into `[25]` gives `[22 | 25]`, not `[25 | 22]`.

## Check yourself

Order 3. Root `[20 | 40]` with leaves `[5 | 12]`, `[25 | 30]` and `[50]`. Where does **45** go, and what does that node look like afterwards?

## Answer

45 is larger than 40, so it goes into the **last leaf**. `[50]` has room, so it becomes `[45 | 50]`.
