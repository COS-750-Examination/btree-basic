## The key idea

Everything about a B-tree of **order m** comes from one number: m is the **most children** a node may have. From that:

- **Most keys** in a node: **m − 1**.
- **Fewest keys** in a node (except the root): **⌈m/2⌉ − 1**.
- A node that is not a leaf with **k keys** has **k + 1 children**.
- The **root** is the exception: it may hold just 1 key.
- **All leaves are on the same level.**

## Worked example

Take **order 5**.

1. Most children: **5**, so most keys: 5 − 1 = **4**.
2. Fewest children for a node that is not the root or a leaf: ⌈5/2⌉ = **3**.
3. Fewest keys for a node that is not the root: 3 − 1 = **2**.

So every node except the root holds **2 to 4 keys**, and an internal node with 3 keys has exactly **4 children**.

## Watch out

- **Order is children, not keys.** Order 3 means up to 3 children, so up to **2** keys.
- **Don't forget the root.** A root with 1 key is fine, even when every other node needs 2.

## Check yourself

In a B-tree of **order 6**, how many keys may a node that is **not the root** hold?

## Answer

Most keys: 6 − 1 = **5**. Fewest: ⌈6/2⌉ − 1 = 3 − 1 = **2**. So **2 to 5 keys**, and an internal node has **3 to 6 children**.
