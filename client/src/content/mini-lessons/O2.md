## The key idea

To decide whether a tree is a valid B-tree, run through a **checklist**. One failure is enough to make it invalid.

1. **Key counts:** every node holds at most m − 1 keys, and every node except the root holds at least ⌈m/2⌉ − 1.
2. **Children:** every internal node with k keys has exactly k + 1 children.
3. **Sorted:** the keys inside each node go from small to large.
4. **Separation:** everything left of a key is smaller, everything right of it is larger, all the way down.
5. **Level:** all leaves are on the same level.

## Worked example

Order 3 (1 to 2 keys per node). Root `[20 | 40]` with children `[10]`, `[30]` and `[35 | 50]`.

1. Key counts: every node has 1 or 2 keys. ✔
2. Children: the root has 2 keys and 3 children. ✔
3. Sorted: ✔
4. Separation: the right child should hold keys **larger than 40**, but it holds **35**. ✘

The tree is **not** a valid B-tree. 35 belongs in the middle child, between 20 and 40.

## Watch out

- **Check separation against every ancestor,** not only the parent. A key deep in the tree must also respect the keys above its parent.
- **Leaves on different levels** make a tree invalid even when every node looks fine on its own.

## Check yourself

Order 3. Root `[15]` with children `[5 | 10 | 12]` and `[20]`. Is it a valid B-tree?

## Answer

**No.** With order 3 a node may hold at most **2** keys, and `[5 | 10 | 12]` holds 3. It has overflowed and should have been split.
